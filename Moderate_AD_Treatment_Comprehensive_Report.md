## 1. Definition & Staging

### 1.1 Clinical Definition of Moderate AD

Moderate Alzheimer's disease is characterized by substantial cognitive, functional, and behavioral impairment, typically defined by:

| Scale | Moderate AD Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **MMSE** | 10–19 (some guidelines: 10–20) | Global cognitive screen, 30-point scale |
| **CDR** | 2.0 (moderate) | Sum-of-Boxes typically 9–15 |
| **FAST** | Stage 6 | Severe ADL impairment |
| **ADAS-Cog** | Score typically 20–40 | Higher = worse |

### 1.2 2024 NIA-AA Revised Staging Criteria

The Alzheimer's Association Workgroup (2024) published revised biology-based criteria (*Alzheimer's & Dementia*, 2024), shifting the definition from syndromal staging to biomarker-confirmed continuum:
- **Stage 3 (Symptomatic)** = Clinical signs present; severity subclassified by biomarker burden and functional impact
- Moderate stage corresponds to **CDR 2** with confirmed amyloid and tau pathology
- Blood-based biomarkers (p-tau217, Aβ42/40 ratio) now included as first-tier diagnostics

### 1.3 Clinical Characteristics at Moderate Stage

- **Cognitive:** Significant episodic memory impairment; disorientation to time and place; language difficulties (word-finding, comprehension); impaired judgment and abstract reasoning
- **Functional:** Loss of ability to perform complex IADLs; may need assistance with basic ADLs (dressing, hygiene); progressive dependency
- **Behavioral:** 60–80% experience BPSD including agitation, wandering, depression, sleep disturbances, paranoia
- **Neurobiological:** Widespread cortical neurodegeneration; significant tau tangle deposition across temporal, parietal, frontal lobes; substantial amyloid burden; severe cholinergic deficit

## 2. Approved Pharmacological Treatments

### 2.1 Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors (AChEIs)

#### Mechanism
AChEIs inhibit the enzyme acetylcholinesterase, preventing degradation of acetylcholine in cholinergic synapses. In AD, cholinergic neurons are preferentially lost, making this mechanism central to symptomatic treatment.

#### Donepezil (Aricept)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Approval** | US FDA: mild, moderate, and severe AD; EU: mild-to-moderate AD |
| **Standard dosing** | Start 5 mg/day × 4–6 weeks → 10 mg/day |
| **Extended dosing** | 23 mg/day formulation approved for moderate-severe AD (US only) |
| **Mechanism** | Selective, reversible AChEI; also blocks NMDA receptors modestly |
| **Evidence** | Multiple Phase 3 RCTs; systematic reviews confirm significant improvement in MMSE, ADAS-Cog, CIBIC-plus vs placebo |

**Key evidence — DOMINO-AD Trial** (*NEJM*, Howard et al., 2012, n=295):
- Patients with moderate-severe AD (SMMSE 5–13) on donepezil ≥3 months randomized to continue vs discontinue
- Continuing donepezil: significantly better SMMSE (+1.9 points, p<0.001) and BADLS scores vs discontinuation at 12 months
- Evidence base: supports **continued donepezil use into moderate-severe stage**

**2024 Meta-Analysis on Donepezil 5 vs 10 mg** (*Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2024):
- 10 mg significantly superior to 5 mg for MMSE (SMD 0.24, 95%CI 0.13–0.35), ADAS-Cog
- Both doses superior to placebo; dose-response relationship confirmed

#### Rivastigmine (Exelon)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Approval** | Mild-to-severe AD (oral and patch); EU/US |
| **Oral dosing** | 1.5 mg bid → titrate to 6 mg bid over 4+ weeks |
| **Standard patch** | 4.6 mg/24h → 9.5 mg/24h |
| **High-dose patch** | 13.3 mg/24h (15 cm²) — for severe AD or inadequate lower-dose response |
| **Mechanism** | Dual inhibitor: both AChE and butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE) |
| **Advantage** | Transdermal delivery avoids GI first-pass; similar efficacy to oral with fewer GI AEs |

**High-Dose Rivastigmine Evidence (ACTION Study + 2025 Review)**:
- 13.3 mg vs 4.6 mg patch in severe AD: significant improvement in ADL (p=0.038) at 24 weeks
- Improvement in 24.6% vs 16.2% of patients; no change/improvement in 58.8% vs 45.4%
- A 2025 expert overview (*Expert Review of Neurotherapeutics*, 2025) confirms the 13.3 mg/24h patch provides incremental functional benefit in patients not responding to lower doses
- Safety: consistent with lower-dose profile; GI AEs slightly higher but manageable with patch formulation

#### Galantamine (Reminyl/Razadyne)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Approval** | Mild-to-moderate AD (US/EU) — NOT approved for severe AD |
| **Dosing** | Extended-release: 8 mg → 16 mg → 24 mg/day over 8+ weeks |
| **Mechanism** | AChEI + allosteric modulator of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChR) |
| **Evidence** | 24 mg dose showed strongest effect on cognitive outcomes in Bayesian network meta-analysis |

**Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis** (*Eur J Clin Pharmacol*, 2020):
- Galantamine 32 mg and 24 mg ranked highest for cognitive function improvement
- Rivastigmine 12 mg ranked highest for clinical global impression
- Rivastigmine 15 cm² patch ranked best for ADLs

#### AChEI Adverse Effects & Management

| Common AE | Frequency | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea/vomiting | 10–20% | Take with food; slower titration; switch to patch |
| Diarrhea | 5–15% | Dose reduction; dietary adjustment |
| Anorexia/weight loss | 5–10% | Monitor weight; nutritional support |
| Bradycardia/syncope | 1–3% | Baseline ECG; caution with SSS/AV block |
| Insomnia/vivid dreams | 5–8% | Morning dosing preferred |
| Muscle cramps | 3–8% | Usually mild and transient |

### 2.2 Memantine (Namenda/Ebixa)

#### Mechanism
Uncompetitive, voltage-dependent antagonist of NMDA-type glutamate receptors. Blocks pathological tonic NMDA activation (excitotoxicity) while preserving physiological synaptic transmission needed for learning and memory. Complementary mechanism to AChEIs.

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Approval** | EU & US: **moderate-to-severe AD** (not mild AD in EU) |
| **Dosing** | 5 mg/day × 1 week → 5 mg bid → 10 mg/15 mg → **10 mg bid** (target) |
| **Extended-release** | 28 mg/day capsule (US) |
| **Evidence level** | "Strongly Recommend" — Clinical Practice Guidelines for Dementia 2025 (PMC11813556) |

**Key Pivotal Trials:**
- **Reisberg et al. (NEJM, 2003)**: Memantine vs placebo in moderate-severe AD; significant improvement in CIBIC-plus (+0.42), ADCS-ADLsev, SIB (p<0.001 all)
- **Multiple Phase 3 RCTs**: Consistent modest but significant benefits on cognition (SIB), global (CIBIC-plus), function (ADCS-ADL), and behavior (NPI)

**CPG Recommendation** (PMC11813556, 2025):
> "Strongly recommend memantine for moderate to severe Alzheimer's disease; significant cognitive and functional improvements demonstrated both independently and alongside cholinesterase inhibitors."

---

### 2.3 Combination Therapy: Donepezil + Memantine

This is the **standard of care** for moderate-to-severe AD in most international guidelines.

#### Mechanistic Rationale
- AChEIs and memantine have different and complementary mechanisms
- No pharmacokinetic interactions between agents
- Additive effects theorized at synaptic and network levels

#### Key Evidence

**1. Tariot et al. (JAMA, 2004)**: First major RCT of combination in moderate-severe AD already on donepezil; adding memantine showed significant SIB (+3.4), CIBIC-plus, ADCS-ADL19, NPI improvements vs placebo (p<0.01 all)

**2. Howard et al. DOMINO-AD (NEJM, 2012)**: n=295 moderate-severe AD
- 4 arms: continue donepezil / stop donepezil / donepezil+memantine / memantine alone
- Continuing donepezil: significant benefit vs stopping (SMMSE +1.9, BADLS p<0.001)
- Adding memantine: **no significant additional benefit vs donepezil alone** on primary outcomes
- Post-hoc analyses suggest benefit in more severe subgroups

**3. 2025 Updated Meta-Analysis** (PMC12205533, 4 RCTs, n=1,930):

| Outcome | Combination vs Donepezil Alone | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| MMSE | OR = 0.54 (95%CI 0.06–4.60) | **Not significant** |
| SIB (Severe Impairment Battery) | OR = 7.00 (95%CI 1.13–43.24) | **Significant** |

**Interpretation**: Combination therapy provides **significant benefit on SIB** (assessing severe cognitive domains) but not MMSE — suggesting **the combination benefits are most evident in more severely impaired patients and deeper cognitive domains**. High heterogeneity (I²=72–89%) warrants caution.

**4. Pooled AUC Analysis** (*Alzheimer's Research & Therapy*, 2015, PMC4436119):
- Combination therapy showed cumulative, additive benefits over individual monotherapies across cognition, function, behavior, and global outcomes
- Effects were consistent and statistically significant over 28 weeks

**Clinical Guidance:**
- In moderate AD: initiate or continue AChEI (donepezil 10 mg preferred) **and** add memantine 10 mg bid
- The combination is standard in US (FDA-approved fixed-dose Namzaric: donepezil 10 mg + memantine ER 28 mg)
- NICE (UK) guidelines also recommend combination for moderate-severe AD

### 2.4 High-Dose Rivastigmine Patch (13.3 mg/24h)

- Specifically approved for **moderate-to-severe AD** in addition to mild AD
- **ACTION Study** (Novartis): high dose superior to 4.6 mg patch on ADL (p=0.038) and global status
- Particularly beneficial for patients with inadequate response to 9.5 mg/24h patch
- **2025 Expert Review** (*Expert Rev Neurother*, 2025): confirms incremental functional benefit; ADL domain most responsive

## 3. Management of Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms (BPSD)

BPSD affects **60–80% of patients with moderate AD** and represents a major driver of caregiver burden and institutionalization. The approach is: **non-pharmacological first → pharmacological only for severe/treatment-resistant cases**.

### 3.1 Agitation and Aggression

#### Brexpiprazole (Rexulti) — First FDA-Approved Agent for AD Agitation

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **FDA Approval** | May 11, 2023 — **first and only drug specifically approved for agitation in AD** |
| **Mechanism** | Serotonin-dopamine activity modulator (SDAM): partial agonist at D2, 5-HT1A; antagonist at 5-HT2A and α1B adrenergic receptors |
| **Dosing** | Day 1–7: 0.5 mg/day → Day 8–14: 1 mg/day → Day 15+: **2 mg/day** (target); max 3 mg/day |
| **Evidence** | 3 pivotal Phase 3 RCTs (n=433 and n=270 primary); significant CMAI-Total improvement at 2 mg vs placebo |
| **Safety** | Somnolence 3.5%, nasopharyngitis 3.1%, dizziness 2.7%; **boxed warning for increased mortality in elderly with dementia-related psychosis** |
| **Status** | Also approved in Canada and Philippines (2024) |

**Pivotal Trial Results:**
- Trial 1 (n=433): 2 mg/day brexpiprazole → significant CMAI improvement (p<0.05) vs placebo
- Trial 2 (n=270): Flexible dosing; benefit driven by patients titrated to 2 mg
- Both trials: NPI-NH Agitation/Aggression subscale also significantly improved

#### Other Agents for Agitation (Off-Label or Under Review)

| Agent | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Citalopram** | Moderate; CITA-AD trial: significant CMAI improvement vs placebo, but QTc prolongation risk | Max 20 mg in AD (vs 40 mg general population); NNT ~6 |
| **Sertraline** | Limited; TRIAD trial: non-significant vs placebo for agitation | May benefit co-occurring depression |
| **Risperidone** | CMAI improvement; only antipsychotic with EU label for AD agitation | NNT ~9; risk: extrapyramidal, CV, ↑mortality |
| **Aripiprazole** | Modest efficacy; better tolerated than older antipsychotics | Off-label; ↑mortality risk (class warning) |
| **Olanzapine** | CATIE-AD: modest benefit; limited by sedation, metabolic AEs | Reserve for severe, acute agitation |
| **Mirtazapine** | **SYMBAD trial (Lancet, 2021): NO benefit + higher mortality** | **Avoid for agitation in AD** |
| **Valproate** | Multiple RCTs: **no benefit over placebo**; liver toxicity, cognitive worsening | **Not recommended** |
| **AXS-05** | Dextromethorphan-bupropion combination; Phase 3 ongoing | Under FDA review for AD agitation |

**Systematic Review (PMC12105158, 2025)** — Key Conclusion:
> "Personalized care strategies tailored to individual patient needs are central to effective agitation management; non-pharmacological interventions offer therapeutic benefits without adverse medication effects."

### 3.2 Depression and Anxiety

Depression affects **30–50%** of moderate AD patients; anxiety affects **20–40%**. The evidence for antidepressants in AD depression is mixed.

**2023 Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis** (PMC10230772):

| SSRI | Effect on Depression in AD | Statistical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Escitalopram** | Significant reduction in depression scores | ✅ Yes |
| **Sertraline** | Significant | ✅ Yes |
| **Paroxetine** | Significant | ✅ Yes |
| **Citalopram** | **NOT effective** for depression in AD | ❌ No |
| **Mirtazapine** | HTA-SADD trial: no significant benefit | ❌ No; also ↑mortality in agitation |

**Clinical Recommendation:**
- First line: psychosocial interventions (activity engagement, meaningful stimulation)
- Pharmacological: escitalopram or sertraline preferred SSRIs for AD depression
- Avoid mirtazapine for BPSD; use with extreme caution for sleep only
- Citalopram: modest use for agitation only (not depression); QTc monitoring required

---

### 3.3 Sleep Disturbance

Sleep disorders (insomnia, circadian dysrhythmia, sundowning) affect **40–70%** of moderate AD patients.

**Evidence-Based Pharmacological Options:**

| Drug | Mechanism | Evidence | RCT Data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suvorexant (Belsomra)** | Orexin receptor antagonist | **Best evidence in AD** | RCT (PMC7984350): significant ↑TST, ↓WASO via PSG; well-tolerated; no cognitive worsening |
| **Trazodone** | 5-HT2 antagonist/SRI | Moderate | RCT (n=30): 50 mg/night ↑TST (+42 min), ↑sleep efficiency; no ADL effect |
| **Melatonin** | Melatonin receptor agonist | Weak — multiple RCTs negative | Cochrane review: no convincing evidence for up to 10 mg |
| **Ramelteon** | Melatonin receptor agonist | Limited | Case series; no robust AD-specific RCT |
| **Low-dose doxepin** | H1 antagonist | General elderly data; not AD-specific | FDAapproved for insomnia but limited AD data |

**Non-pharmacological for sleep (strong evidence):**
- Structured light therapy (bright light exposure in morning): circadian re-entrainment
- Sleep hygiene program: regular sleep/wake schedule, activity-rest cycles
- Tailored lighting (individualized spectrum/intensity): CMAI reductions + improved sleep

---

### 3.4 Psychosis (Hallucinations, Delusions, Paranoia)

- Affects **20–30%** of moderate AD patients
- **Off-label use of low-dose atypical antipsychotics** (risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole)
- **ALL antipsychotics carry FDA Black Box Warning**: ~1.6–1.7× increased mortality vs placebo in elderly dementia patients
- Use only when: (1) non-pharmacological measures fail; (2) symptoms cause significant distress or safety risk; (3) informed consent obtained from caregiver
- **Pimavanserin** (FDA-approved for Parkinson's disease psychosis): Phase 3 trial in AD psychosis (HARMONY) failed primary endpoint; not approved for AD
- **Quetiapine**: widely used off-label for mixed BPSD despite limited efficacy evidence

## 4. Non-Pharmacological Interventions

Non-pharmacological interventions (NPIs) are **recommended as first-line treatment** for most BPSD and as adjuncts to pharmacotherapy for cognitive and functional maintenance.

### 4.1 Exercise Therapy

**Best-Evidenced NPI for Cognitive Function in AD**

**Evidence Base:** Meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (PMC12183972, 2025):
- Exercise was the top-performing NPI across MMSE, ADL, and NPI outcomes
- MMSE: SMD = 0.65; ADL: MD = −2.30; NPI: MD = −2.16 (all significant)

**Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis (2025, 57 RCTs, n=6,737):**
- Aerobic exercise ranked #1 for global cognition (probability 79.5%)
- Exercise therapy SUCRA score: 40.8% (MMSE-based network); significant SMD = 0.28

**Types of Exercise Studied:**

| Type | Benefit | Best Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Aerobic exercise** (walking, cycling, swimming) | Global cognition, processing speed, executive function | ★★★★ Strongest |
| **Resistance/strength training** | Executive function, ADLs, QoL | ★★★ Strong |
| **Multi-component exercise** (aerobic + resistance + balance) | Superior to either alone when ≥4 months duration | ★★★★ Strongest |
| **Tai Chi/Qigong** | Balance, cognition, fall prevention | ★★★ Strong |

**EXERT Study** (*Alzheimer's & Dementia*, 2025, Baker et al.): Structured aerobic exercise in MCI → favorable amyloid/tau biomarker changes; further validates neurobiological mechanism.

**Recommendation:** 
> Structured exercise 150 min/week (moderate-intensity aerobic) + 2× resistance training; supervised program > home-based; begin with tailored safety assessment.

---

### 4.2 Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Structured group or individual activities engaging mental processes (memory, language, problem-solving) |
| **Evidence** | 2025 Bayesian NMA: SUCRA 48.9%; SMD = 0.32 vs control (significant) |
| **Protocol** | Typically 14 sessions (45 min each, twice weekly × 7 weeks); evidence-based manual exists (Spector et al.) |
| **Outcomes** | MMSE, ADAS-Cog improvement; also QoL, communication, self-esteem |
| **Cost-effective** | Group format → scalable; recommended by NICE for mild-moderate AD |

---

### 4.3 Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence ranking** | 2025 meta-analysis: 2nd best NPI (after exercise); significant MMSE (SMD 0.65 combined with exercise) |
| **Protocol** | Most studies: 10–20 Hz, DLPFC (left or bilateral), 1600–2000 pulses/session, 10–20 sessions |
| **Specific finding** | rTMS + cognitive training > rTMS alone; significant reductions in NPI, CMAI |
| **Mechanism** | Modulates cortical excitability; enhances neural plasticity; emerging evidence for glymphatic activation |
| **Safety** | Generally safe; rare seizure risk (<1/10,000 sessions); contraindicated with metallic cranial implants |
| **Regulatory** | Several rTMS devices CE-marked/cleared for AD in some jurisdictions; FDA clearance for MDD; AD indication in development |

**Key 2024 Trial Data**: rTMS combined with cognitive training → significant reductions in neuropsychiatric behaviors including agitation, and cognitive improvements in MMSE; also improved ADL function.

---

### 4.4 Photobiomodulation (PBM) / 40 Hz Gamma Sensory Stimulation

**Emerging Evidence — Currently the Highest-Ranked NPI for Cognition in 2025 Network Meta-Analysis**

#### Photobiomodulation (Transcranial/Systemic PBM)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SUCRA Ranking** | #1 in 2025 Bayesian NMA (57 RCTs, 6,737 participants): 87.3% SUCRA score |
| **SMD vs control** | 0.66 (95%CrI 0.29–1.02) — highest of all interventions |
| **Mechanism** | Near-infrared light (810–1064 nm) penetrates skull → enhances mitochondrial cytochrome C oxidase activity; reduces oxidative stress; anti-inflammatory effects |
| **Evidence quality** | 7 RCTs included; effect size consistent but study sizes small |

#### 40 Hz Gamma Sensory Stimulation (GENUS — Gamma Entrainment Using Sensory Stimuli)

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | 40 Hz flickering light + tone → entrain gamma oscillations → activate microglial amyloid clearance, reduce neuroinflammation, preserve neural circuits |
| **Pilot results (n=15, mild AD)** | Significant: ↓ventricular dilation, ↓hippocampal atrophy, ↑default mode network connectivity, ↑face-name recall, ↑activity rhythmicity vs sham at 3 months |
| **Long-term (30 months)** | Some participants maintained benefits; female patients appeared more responsive; small sample (n=5) |
| **2024 Phase 2A RCT** | Safety and tolerability confirmed in mild-moderate AD; cognitive benefit signals present |
| **Ongoing Phase 3** | Cognito Therapeutics conducting larger trial (NCT status: recruiting globally) |
| **Safety** | Non-invasive; well-tolerated; no serious adverse events reported |

**Critical Note**: GENUS and PBM results are promising but require larger Phase 3 confirmation before becoming standard of care.

### 4.5 Music Therapy

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Types** | Active (playing instruments, singing) vs receptive (listening to music) |
| **Systematic review 2025 (PMC12235852)** | Significant benefits on cognition (memory, attention, language), BPSD (anxiety, depression, agitation), QoL |
| **Bayesian NMA 2025** | SUCRA 32.9%; SMD = 0.22 — not statistically significant in all analyses |
| **Meta-analysis of 17 NPI RCTs (PMC12183972)** | "Music therapy did not seem to improve cognitive functioning" as a standalone NPI |
| **Best use case** | Personalized/individualized music for agitation; emotionally meaningful repertoire > generic music |
| **Conclusion** | Beneficial for BPSD (particularly agitation, depression, anxiety) more than pure cognition; excellent safety; low cost |

### 4.6 Enriched Environment (EE) / Sensory Stimulation

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SUCRA Ranking** | #2 in 2025 Bayesian NMA: 83.8%; SMD = 0.69 (significant) |
| **Components** | Multisensory stimulation rooms (Snoezelen), personalized sensory items, nature exposure, social enrichment |

### 4.7 Multi-Domain Lifestyle Interventions (FINGER-Type)

Building on the FINGER trial (Finland), multi-domain RCTs targeting multiple risk factors simultaneously show greater effect than single-domain approaches.

| Study | Population | Interventions | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FINGER** (Lancet, 2015) | MCI risk population | Diet + exercise + cognitive training + vascular monitoring | 25% better global cognition vs control at 2 years |
| **HERITAGE** (NCT05886114, Zhejiang, 2023–2027) | At-risk community elderly, n=1,200 | Same 4-domain approach | Ongoing; primary cognitive outcomes pending |
| **MIND-China, EXERT, MAPT** | Various | Multi-domain | Consistent signal toward cognitive protection |

**Evidence-Based Recommendation**: Multi-domain interventions including at minimum exercise + cognitive stimulation + vascular risk management are the most potent non-pharmacological approach.

## 5. Caregiver Interventions

Moderate AD places intense demands on family caregivers. Caregiver burden is directly linked to **patient behavioral outcomes, institutionalization rates, and quality of care**.

### 5.1 Evidence for Caregiver Interventions

**2025 Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis** (*BMC Geriatrics*, 2025, PMC12437130) on multi-component support programs:
- Multi-component programs integrating **education + psychoeducation + social support** showed the most positive outcomes on caregiver burden
- Programs requiring active caregiver participation had the broadest effects

**Psychoeducation Meta-Analysis** (PMC11693363, 2025):
- 23 articles, n=2,651 caregivers
- 11/23 studies: significant ↓caregiver burden
- 8/23: significant ↓depression
- 4/23: significant ↓anxiety
- Effect sizes small-to-moderate but clinically meaningful

**Effects on care recipients** when caregivers receive training:
- ↓neuropsychiatric symptoms, ↑cognition, ↑QoL
- **Delayed institutionalization** (care coordination + psychoeducation programs)

### 5.2 Evidence-Based Caregiver Intervention Components

| Component | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Disease education | ★★★★ | Essential for all caregivers |
| Behavioral management training | ★★★★ | Strong; specifically reduces BPSD |
| Respite care | ★★★ | Reduces acute caregiver burnout |
| Psychotherapy (CBT for caregivers) | ★★★ | Reduces caregiver depression/anxiety |
| Online/digital interventions | ★★★ | Increasing evidence; meta-analysis 2025 |
| Peer support groups | ★★★ | Social support; information sharing |
| Care coordination/case management | ★★★★ | Most potent for delaying institutionalization |

### 5.3 Digital and Online Caregiver Programs

2025 meta-analysis (*AJGP*, 2024) on online dementia caregiver interventions:
- Comparable effectiveness to in-person for burden reduction
- Significantly more accessible; high adherence when structured

## 7. Sodium Oligomannate (GV-971)

**Special Section: Chinese-Approved Agent**

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Brand name** | Mannite (满思唤，绿谷制药) |
| **Approved in China** | November 2019 — mild-to-moderate AD, cognitive improvement |
| **Mechanism** | Marine algae-derived oligosaccharide; remodels gut microbiota → ↓peripheral inflammation → ↓neuroinflammation; also directly interferes with Aβ aggregation |
| **Phase 3 Trial (China, 2021)** | 36-week, double-blind, n=818 mild-to-moderate AD; significant ↑ADAS-Cog-12 vs placebo (primary endpoint met, p<0.001); effect maintained throughout |
| **Global Phase 3 FORTUNE** | Started 2021; **terminated early** May 2022 after enrolling 439/1,218 patients — regulatory and operational reasons cited, not safety/futility signal |
| **Current status in China** | Approved and marketed; widely prescribed for mild-moderate AD |
| **Controversy** | FDA advisory panel expressed skepticism about trial design; mechanism of action remains debated internationally |

## 9. Conclusions & Treatment Algorithm

### 9.1 Summary of Evidence Tiers

**Tier 1 — Strong Evidence, Recommended:**
- Donepezil 10 mg/day ± extended-release formulation
- Memantine 20 mg/day (ER 28 mg/day)
- **Combination donepezil + memantine** (standard of care for moderate AD)
- Rivastigmine 9.5–13.3 mg/24h patch (especially if GI intolerance)
- Structured aerobic exercise (150 min/week)
- Cognitive stimulation therapy (14-session program)
- Caregiver psychoeducation + behavioral management training

**Tier 2 — Moderate Evidence, Clinically Useful:**
- Brexpiprazole 2 mg/day (for moderate-severe agitation)
- rTMS (20 Hz, L-DLPFC, 10 sessions)
- Suvorexant for sleep disorders
- Escitalopram/sertraline for comorbid depression
- Group music therapy for BPSD management
- Multi-domain lifestyle intervention

**Tier 3 — Emerging, Promising but Insufficient Phase 3 Data:**
- 40 Hz gamma sensory stimulation (GENUS)
- Photobiomodulation
- KarXT (muscarinic agonist)
- Masitinib
- GLP-1 agonists

### 9.2 Step-Wise Treatment Algorithm for Moderate AD

```
MODERATE AD CONFIRMED (CDR=2; MMSE 10-19)
│
├─► STEP 1: Establish pharmacological backbone
│   ├── Start/continue AChEI (donepezil 10mg preferred)
│   └── Add memantine 20mg/day → [COMBINATION = STANDARD OF CARE]
│
├─► STEP 2: Non-pharmacological — INTEGRATE INTO ALL CARE PLANS
│   ├── Structured exercise program (aerobic + multi-component)
│   ├── Cognitive stimulation therapy (group preferred)
│   ├── Caregiver education + behavioral training
│   └── Environmental optimization (lighting, sensory enrichment)
│
├─► STEP 3: Assess and treat BPSD
│   ├── Agitation: Non-pharmacological first → if severe: brexpiprazole
│   ├── Depression/anxiety: Escitalopram or sertraline
│   ├── Sleep: Sleep hygiene → suvorexant → trazodone
│   └── Psychosis: Risperidone (lowest effective dose, short-term)
│
├─► STEP 4: Consider emerging/adjunct therapies
│   ├── rTMS (10 sessions, DLPFC)
│   ├── GV-971 (China-approved mild-moderate AD)
│   └── 40Hz sensory stimulation (as adjunct if available)
│
└─► STEP 5: Caregiver and care system
    ├── Regular caregiver burden assessment (ZBI)
    ├── Psychoeducation program + peer support
    ├── Respite care planning
    └── Advanced care planning for transition to severe stage
```
