# Healthcare Reimbursement Research Report: Wearable and App-Connected Health Devices for Quality-of-Life Indications

**Prepared:** June 2026 | **Focus:** Menstruation/Menopause, Sleep/Insomnia, Digestive Discomfort, Stress/Fatigue/Recovery

---

## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Theme 1 — The Reimbursement Gap Is Real and Structural**
Consumer wellness wearables (Oura, Whoop, Fitbit, Garmin, Apple Watch) and most women's health and lifestyle apps currently receive no direct insurance reimbursement from traditional payers such as Medicare, Medicaid, or commercial plans. The payer system distinguishes sharply between medical devices that diagnose and treat clinically defined conditions and wellness products that support healthy behavior. The four most realistic near-term revenue paths for a startup in these spaces are: (1) direct-to-consumer self-pay, (2) HSA/FSA spending (where eligibility has significantly expanded — Flo, Clue, Oura Ring, and Whoop are now eligible), (3) employer Lifestyle Spending Accounts (LSAs), and (4) employer benefit platforms such as Carrot Fertility, Maven Clinic, and Progyny, which now explicitly cover menopause, perimenopause, and related tracking tools under employer-funded benefits. Traditional insurance coverage for a new wearable targeting quality-of-life is a multi-year, evidence-intensive undertaking.

**Theme 2 — Digital Mental Health Therapeutics Have a Real Medicare Pathway, But It Is Narrow**
Effective January 1, 2025, CMS established three new HCPCS codes — G0552, G0553, and G0554 — for Digital Mental Health Treatment (DMHT) devices. This is the first structured Medicare reimbursement pathway for software-delivered therapy. To qualify, a product must be FDA-cleared for computerized behavioral therapy for a psychiatric disorder, prescribed by a licensed clinician, and the clinician must supervise ongoing care. SleepioRx (CBT-I for insomnia) and DaylightRx (CBT for generalized anxiety) are among the seven products that currently qualify. The reimbursement rates are modest (G0553 ~$51.76 for first 20 minutes of management; G0554 ~$39.79 per additional 20 minutes; G0552 at contractor pricing), and the "buy-and-bill" model requires the provider to front the cost. This pathway is potentially applicable to a clinician-supervised sleep insomnia CBT product, but is not available for menopause wearables, gut health apps, or stress/recovery trackers unless CBT for a defined psychiatric disorder is the core mechanism.

**Theme 3 — RPM Is a Real Pathway for Clinician-Supervised Physiologic Monitoring, but Faces Headwinds**
Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM) codes (99453, 99454, 99457, 99458) offer a legitimate billing pathway for FDA-cleared devices that automatically transmit physiologic data as part of a clinician-managed care program. The 2026 CMS fee schedule updated the minimum monitoring requirement from 16 to 2 days and introduced CPT 99470 for shorter monitoring windows (~$26/month). However, UnitedHealthcare announced plans to restrict RPM coverage to only heart failure and hypertension-in-pregnancy starting in 2026 (delayed, not reversed), signaling significant commercial payer pushback. Medicare fee-for-service remains broadly supportive of RPM, but commercial coverage is fragmenting. RPM is realistic for sleep apnea, hypertension, and cardiac monitoring but is not currently a viable pathway for menopause tracking, IBS management, or general stress/recovery use cases under most payer policies.

**Theme 4 — Menopause Telehealth Has Achieved Insurance Coverage; Wearables Have Not**
A clear split has emerged: menopause telehealth platforms (Midi Health, Gennev, Elektra Health) have secured insurance contracts with Aetna, Anthem, UnitedHealthcare, and BCBS plans, covering clinician visits and HRT prescriptions as E&M services under standard billing codes. The wearable or monitoring component (e.g., a hot-flash sensor, a temperature tracker) is not separately reimbursed — but it can be bundled into the telehealth service as a clinical data collection tool and included in the subscription. Embr Wave launched an "Embrship" subscription model ($20/month) rather than pursuing insurance. Nerivio (a prescription wearable for migraine, not menopause) demonstrates that a prescription, FDA-cleared wearable can achieve broad insurance coverage (~130 million lives) through pharmacy benefit pathways and DME policies, providing the clearest blueprint for a wearable targeting a defined medical condition.

**Theme 5 — Prescription Digital Therapeutics Legislation Is Advancing, But US Reimbursement Outside Mental Health Remains Embryonic**
The Access to Prescription Digital Therapeutics Act (H.R. 3288 / S. 1702), reintroduced in May 2025, would establish Medicare and Medicaid benefit categories for FDA-authorized PDTs and require CMS to create product-specific HCPCS codes within two years. It has not yet passed. In the interim, the only US pathway with specific PDT reimbursement codes is the DMHT framework (psychiatric disorders only). Mahana's Cara Care for IBS secured permanent reimbursement in Germany at €248/course (via the DiGA pathway), but has no comparable US reimbursement. Pear Therapeutics' bankruptcy in 2023 after achieving FDA clearance but failing to achieve commercial-scale insurance reimbursement stands as the clearest cautionary tale: FDA clearance alone does not create a reimbursement pathway, and payer coverage must be built through clinician engagement, real-world outcomes data, and extensive contracting.

## SECTION 2: REIMBURSEMENT PATHWAY MAP

### 2A. Menstruation and Menopause (Cycle Trackers, Hot Flash Wearables, Temperature Trackers, Hormone Apps)

**Self-Pay (Immediate):** Core revenue model for all consumer products. Flo Premium ~$40-100/year; Natural Cycles ~$120/year; Embr Wave ~$250 device or $20/month subscription; Tempdrop ~$150; Ava Bracelet ~$300.

**HSA/FSA (Immediate/Available Now):** Flo Premium became HSA/FSA-eligible in October 2024. Clue Plus became eligible January 15, 2025. Natural Cycles is potentially ACA-eligible as a contraceptive method (covered at zero cost-sharing by commercial plans under ACA). Tempdrop and Ava (fertility monitoring) may qualify with a Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN). TENS devices like Livia are HSA/FSA eligible as pain management devices.

**Employer Benefits (Near-Term):** Carrot Fertility covers menopause care including HRT access, virtual clinician visits, and now integrates Oura Ring wearable data in its Sprints metabolic/perimenopause program (announced May 2026). Maven Clinic's Menopause & Ongoing Care is the company's fastest-growing benefit, with 550+ employer clients. Ovia Health expanded to menopause symptom tracking in 2024. Lifestyle Spending Accounts (LSAs) at employers who design them broadly can cover Oura Ring, Tempdrop, and similar devices.

**Clinic Bundle / Telehealth Subscription (Near-Term):** Gennev is in-network with Aetna, Anthem, and UnitedHealthcare. Midi Health accepts Aetna, BCBS, and UHC in all 50 states. Elektra Health covers commercial and government plans in select states. Insurance coverage is for the clinician visit (E/M codes 99201-99215, preventive codes 99384-99397), not the monitoring device itself. A startup can use a wearable as a clinical data layer in these telehealth models, bundling device cost into subscription or visit fees.

**RPM/RTM (Complex, Conditional):** Technically applicable if the wearable tracks a physiologic parameter (e.g., skin temperature, HRV) as part of a clinician-managed chronic care program for a coded condition (e.g., N95.1 menopausal climacteric states). No currently established RPM pathway exists specifically for menopause monitoring, and commercial payer coverage for RPM in this indication does not exist today. A 2026 CMS update reduced minimum monitoring days to 2, making RPM more practical for episodic monitoring.

**Traditional Insurance (Long-Term, Unlikely for Device Alone):** No commercial payer currently covers menopause wearables as a standalone benefit. Hormone testing is covered with diagnosis codes but salivary hormone testing is considered investigational. A prescription TENS device for dysmenorrhea has a potential DME pathway but no current coverage.

**PDT Reimbursement (Long-Term, Possible via DMHT if Behavioral):** If the product delivers CBT-based intervention for insomnia or anxiety symptoms in menopausal women, the DMHT codes (G0552-G0554) may apply. The PDT Act, if enacted, could create a broader pathway.

### 2B. Sleep and Insomnia (Wearable Trackers, CBT-I Apps, Clinician-Supervised Programs)

**Self-Pay:** Oura Ring ~$300-400 device plus $6/month; Whoop 4.0 ~$239/year; Sleepio consumer subscription ~$400/year (historically); SleepioRx as prescribed product.

**HSA/FSA:** Oura Ring is eligible; Whoop is eligible; Apple Watch with medical necessity documentation is eligible; sleep apps with an LMN may qualify.

**Employer Benefits:** LSAs cover Oura, Whoop; Spring Health and Lyra Health (employer mental health platforms) may include CBT-I apps in their networks. Employer sleep programs are an emerging benefit category.

**Clinic Bundle/Telehealth:** Sleep telehealth platforms offering clinician-supervised CBT-I can bill under behavior change/psychotherapy codes. Home sleep testing (HST) for sleep apnea is well-covered (CPT 95800, 95801) and allows wearable-adjacent devices.

**RPM/RTM:** Sleep is not explicitly covered under current RTM codes (which address musculoskeletal/respiratory). However, if a device monitors respiratory parameters (SpO2, respiratory rate) as part of sleep apnea management, RTM code 98976 (respiratory) is applicable. For sleep apnea in particular, UHC's proposed RPM restriction would eliminate coverage (it listed sleep apnea as excluded).

**DMHT/PDT (Active Pathway):** SleepioRx and other FDA-cleared CBT-I products now qualify for Medicare reimbursement under G0552-G0554 (2025). This is the most validated US reimbursement pathway currently available for a sleep digital therapeutic. Requirements: FDA 510(k) clearance for chronic insomnia, prescription required, licensed clinician must manage, "buy-and-bill" model for G0552. Sleepio is also available through CVS Caremark pharmacy benefit, offering an alternative pharmacy channel.

**Traditional Insurance:** Cigna has established medical coverage policy MM-0565 for PDTs with criteria-based coverage. BCBS added G0552-G0554 effective January 2025. Commercial coverage for CBT-I PDTs is growing but still requires prior authorization, FDA clearance, and prescription status.

### 2C. Digestive Discomfort (IBS Apps, Gut Health Apps, Food/Diet Trackers)

**Self-Pay:** Primary model for all gut health apps in the US. Nerva (hypnotherapy for IBS) ~$80 for a 3-month program; Cara Care app (premium) ~$50/month; Mahana IBS (previously) was self-pay in the US.

**HSA/FSA:** Gut health apps without medical necessity documentation are generally not HSA/FSA-eligible. With a Letter of Medical Necessity for IBS, it is possible but not guaranteed. General nutrition/diet trackers are not eligible.

**Employer Benefits:** Some large employer benefit platforms (e.g., Included Health, Transcarent) cover GI care coordination. GI-specific digital therapeutics are not yet mainstream in employer benefit catalogs in the US.

**Clinic Bundle/Telehealth:** GI telehealth with clinician supervision can bill standard E/M codes (99201-99215) with IBS diagnosis codes (K58.0 IBS-D, K58.1 IBS-C, K58.9 unspecified). Chronic Care Management codes (99490, 99491) are applicable for patients with diagnosed IBS who receive ongoing monthly management.

**RPM/RTM:** No established RTM/RPM pathway for GI conditions. RTM codes (98975-98981) cover musculoskeletal and respiratory systems only.

**Germany DiGA pathway (Reference):** Mahana's Cara Care for IBS received permanent reimbursement in Germany at €248/90-day course through the DiGA pathway (statutory health insurer reimbursement) in November 2023, confirmed June 2024. This has no direct US equivalent today.

**Traditional Insurance / PDT (Long-Term):** No US payer currently reimburses a standalone IBS digital therapeutic. The DMHT pathway is restricted to psychiatric disorders. The PDT Act, if enacted, could enable a pathway for FDA-cleared GI apps. This is the most challenging category for US reimbursement.

### 2D. Stress, Fatigue, and Recovery (HRV, Readiness, Oura/Whoop-Type Wearables)

**Self-Pay:** Primary model. Oura Ring Gen4 ~$350-400; Whoop 4.0 annual subscription ~$239; Garmin health suite embedded in watch; Apple Watch Ultra ~$799. These are predominantly consumer wellness products.

**HSA/FSA:** Oura Ring is explicitly FSA/HSA eligible. Whoop qualifies under most plans. Apple Watch requires LMN. Garmin and Fitbit require LMN or are ineligible at standard administrators. HRV-focused use for managing a diagnosed condition (e.g., anxiety, cardiac arrhythmia, post-COVID syndrome) strengthens LMN justification.

**LSA/Employer Wellness:** Most direct pathway. Oura, Whoop, Apple Watch, Garmin all eligible under LSA programs designed to include wellness technology. Essence Healthcare (Medicare Advantage) offered free Oura Rings to select members starting 2025. Progyny partnered with Oura in July 2025 to integrate wearable data for fertility-to-menopause care.

**RPM (Conditional):** An Oura Ring or equivalent that automatically transmits physiologic data (heart rate, HRV, SpO2, temperature) as part of a clinician-managed program for a defined chronic condition could theoretically support RPM billing. However, RPM requires the device to be presented as a medical device (FDA-cleared), data must auto-transmit, and billing must be tied to a chronic condition. No consumer wearable has been covered under RPM as a standalone general wellness product.

**Traditional Insurance (Unlikely):** No insurer covers Oura, Whoop, Garmin, or Apple Watch wellness features as standalone reimbursed benefits. The concept of "readiness" or "recovery score" has no ICD-10 analog that would trigger medical necessity.

---

### 2E. Clinician-Supervised Remote Care (RPM/RTM, Telehealth, Digital Therapeutics)

This is the highest-revenue, highest-barrier pathway. Key entry points:

- **RPM programs** tied to hypertension, CHF, diabetes, COPD remain the most established Medicare-covered pathways, with 99453-99458 codes well-established.
- **RTM programs** tied to musculoskeletal (98977) and respiratory (98976) conditions offer clinical staff billing, lower physician involvement, and applicability to physical therapy contexts.
- **Chronic Care Management (CCM)** codes 99490/99491/99439 provide monthly management billing for any complex chronic condition, applicable to IBS, menopausal symptoms, anxiety-related insomnia — with any digital tool as a data source.
- **DMHT codes** G0552-G0554 create the first psychiatry/behavioral health PDT billing pathway under Medicare (2025+).
- **Employer benefit platforms** (Maven, Carrot, Progyny, Spring Health, Lyra) provide B2B2C channels that bypass traditional insurance contracting while creating scalable revenue.

## SECTION 5: PAYER POLICY SUMMARY

### CMS/Medicare

**(a) Consumer wearables:** Not covered as standalone devices. No Medicare benefit category for general wellness wearables. Essence Healthcare (Medicare Advantage) providing free Oura Rings is a supplemental benefit, not traditional Medicare.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Menopause care visits covered under E&M codes with N95.x diagnosis. HRT prescriptions covered under Part D. Hormone testing coverage limited (FSH, LH, estradiol for symptom evaluation covered; salivary testing excluded). No coverage for hot-flash wearables, cycle tracking apps, or menopause monitoring devices.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** DMHT codes G0552-G0554 effective January 1, 2025 cover FDA-cleared CBT-I products like SleepioRx. Home sleep testing for apnea (95800, 95801) well-covered. Polysomnography (95810, 95811) covered. Consumer sleep trackers not covered.

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No specific PDT pathway. IBS management billed via E&M and CCM codes with K58.x diagnosis. No coverage for gut health apps, food trackers, or microbiome tools.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** Not covered. RPM codes require medical-grade FDA-cleared devices with clinician oversight for specific chronic conditions. Wellness wearables tracking HRV, recovery, readiness scores have no Medicare benefit category.

**Key 2026 Medicare updates:** New RPM code 99470 (10-minute management, ~$26); reduced minimum monitoring days to 2 for 99453/99454. CY 2026 final rule signals continued expansion of digital health coverage in proposed rulemaking.

---

### UnitedHealthcare (UHC)

**(a) Consumer wearables:** Not covered. Wearable data may be incorporated into care coordination platforms but no direct reimbursement.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Virtual menopause visits covered through in-network providers (Gennev, Midi Health in-network). No coverage for monitoring devices.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** Under proposed 2026 policy: sleep apnea RPM would be excluded from coverage. CBT-I apps: no formal commercial coverage policy for SleepioRx under DMHT framework (Medicare codes don't automatically create commercial coverage).

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No coverage.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** Not covered. UHC has partnered with Virgin Pulse and has an individualized digital health platform for 11 million members tracking wellness metrics, but this is a wellness program, not a reimbursed medical benefit.

**Critical development:** UHC announced plans to restrict RPM coverage to heart failure and pregnancy hypertension only (effective 2026, currently delayed). This is the most restrictive commercial payer RPM policy announced to date and signals broader commercial RPM coverage erosion.

---

### Aetna

**(a) Consumer wearables:** Not covered as standalone medical benefits. Provides member discounts on fitness devices and offers some supplemental fitness benefits. Has a Clinical Policy Bulletin (CPB 0999) on prescription digital therapeutics requiring FDA clearance, clinician prescription, and peer-reviewed evidence of clinical benefit.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Gennev and Midi Health are in-network. Aetna was one of the first to cover intrauterine insemination as a medical benefit (2024). Menopause telehealth visits covered as E&M. No coverage for monitoring wearables.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** No formal commercial PDT coverage for CBT-I announced. DMHT codes are Medicare-specific; Aetna's commercial adoption is pending. CPB 0999 sets criteria that a CBT-I product could potentially meet.

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No coverage.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** Provides discounts and supports wellness programs; no reimbursement.

---

### Cigna/Evernorth

**(a) Consumer wearables:** Not covered. Has medical coverage policy MM-0565 for prescription digital therapeutics that requires FDA authorization and published clinical evidence. Policy provides a framework that qualifying products could use.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Menopause telehealth covered via E&M codes in-network. No wearable coverage.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** MM-0565 could apply to a CBT-I PDT if criteria are met. No confirmed commercial coverage for SleepioRx or equivalent as of research date. Partnered with CVS Caremark/Sleepio for employer plan clients.

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No coverage.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** Partnered with Virgin Pulse on wellness platform for 11 million members; this is a wellness engagement program, not clinical reimbursement.

---

### Blue Cross Blue Shield (BCBS)

**(a) Consumer wearables:** BCBS Mississippi policy explicitly defines covered digital health technologies as "Software as a Medical Device" that has received FDA clearance and is prescribed by a healthcare provider. Consumer wellness wearables do not meet this definition.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Menopause telehealth covered with in-network providers. BCBS added G0552-G0554 DMHT codes effective January 2025 for Medicare beneficiaries. BCBS Massachusetts published a positive reimbursement policy for Nerivio (migraine wearable) covering 3+ million members in 2025 — precedent for prescription wearable coverage.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** BCBS added G0552-G0554; coverage for CBT-I PDTs like SleepioRx advancing. Prior authorization likely required.

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No coverage.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** No coverage. LSA/wellness programs at some BCBS-affiliated employers may reimburse wearable purchases through lifestyle benefit buckets.

### Medicaid

**(a) Consumer wearables:** Not covered by any state Medicaid program as a standalone benefit.

**(b) Menopause/menstrual tools:** Menopause-related E&M services covered when medically necessary. Coverage varies by state. DMHT codes will eventually propagate to Medicaid states following Medicare adoption; timeline uncertain.

**(c) Sleep digital therapeutics:** Pear Therapeutics' products (reSET, reSET-O) were covered by select state Medicaid programs before bankruptcy — showing Medicaid can adopt digital therapeutics, but often requires state-specific agreements. SleepioRx Medicaid coverage is not yet established.

**(d) Digestive health apps:** No coverage in any state.

**(e) Lifestyle/wellness wearables:** Not covered. The PDT Act, if passed, would extend Medicaid coverage for FDA-authorized PDTs.

## SECTION 6: REIMBURSEMENT LIKELIHOOD BY INDICATION

### Menstruation (Cycle Tracking, Dysmenorrhea, Fertility Monitoring)

**Current reimbursement status:** Predominantly self-pay. FSA/HSA eligible for apps (Flo, Clue since 2024-2025). ACA contraceptive coverage for Natural Cycles. No insurance coverage for devices (Tempdrop, Ava). TENS devices (Livia) are HSA/FSA eligible but not insured.

**Likelihood rating: UNLIKELY for device/app; POSSIBLE for ACA contraceptive category (Natural Cycles model)**

**Requirements to achieve reimbursement:** For a TENS/pain device: FDA 510(k) clearance, clinical evidence of dysmenorrhea pain reduction, DME billing code (category does not exist currently), advocate for payer-specific medical necessity policy. For a cycle-tracking app: FDA clearance as contraceptive (De Novo pathway, 2+ year process) enables ACA no-cost coverage.

**Realistic timeline:** HSA/FSA: immediate (6-12 months to implement). Insurance DME coverage for menstrual pain device: 5-7 years. ACA contraceptive coverage pathway: 3-5 years after FDA clearance.

---

### Menopause/Hot Flashes/Perimenopause Temperature Tracking

**Current reimbursement status:** Telehealth visits for menopause management are insurance-covered (E&M codes). Hormone testing partially covered. Wearables and monitoring devices not reimbursed. Employer benefit platforms (Carrot, Maven, Progyny) are the primary scalable channel for menopause digital health beyond self-pay.

**Likelihood rating: UNLIKELY for wearable device; UNCERTAIN for app integrated into clinician-managed program; LIKELY for telehealth visit model**

**Requirements to achieve reimbursement:** Clinician-supervised model using wearable data as clinical input; integration into RPM framework with FDA-cleared device and a codeable chronic condition (N95.1); publication of RCT-level evidence demonstrating clinically meaningful outcomes (symptom burden, QoL scores like MRS, Greene Climacteric Scale); payer medical policy negotiation.

**Realistic timeline:** Employer benefit: 6-18 months. Telehealth-bundled model: 12-24 months. RPM pathway: 3-5 years. Insurance reimbursement for standalone wearable: 7-10 years.

---

### Sleep/Insomnia (CBT-I, Wearable Sleep Monitoring, Clinician-Supervised Programs)

**Current reimbursement status:** CBT-I PDTs: Medicare-reimbursed via G0552-G0554 (2025+); SleepioRx and DaylightRx are live. Home sleep testing (95800, 95801): well-covered for sleep apnea diagnosis. Consumer sleep trackers: not covered. Apple Watch sleep apnea detection FDA-cleared but not reimbursable.

**Likelihood rating: LIKELY for FDA-cleared CBT-I PDT with prescription model; UNCERTAIN for wearable sleep monitor; UNLIKELY for consumer sleep tracker**

**Requirements to achieve reimbursement:** CBT-I PDT: FDA 510(k) clearance for chronic insomnia disorder, RCT evidence (PSQI, ISI validated scores), prescription model, licensed clinician management, buy-and-bill or pharmacy benefit model. Wearable sleep monitor: FDA clearance for sleep assessment, clinician integration, published evidence of clinical benefit vs. PSG or HST.

**Realistic timeline:** CBT-I PDT with DMHT codes: 2-4 years if evidence exists and FDA clearance is secured. Commercial payer coverage expansion of DMHT framework: 2-5 years. Consumer tracker insurance: unlikely in 10-year horizon.

---

### Digestive/IBS-Like Symptoms (Gut Health Apps, Bloating, Dietary Tracking)

**Current reimbursement status:** No US payer currently reimburses any standalone GI digital therapeutic or gut health wearable. Germany's DiGA pathway has been achieved (Cara Care for IBS). US billing depends on clinician-supervised models using E&M and CCM codes.

**Likelihood rating: UNLIKELY in the near term for any standalone app or wearable**

**Requirements to achieve reimbursement:** FDA 510(k) or De Novo clearance for IBS treatment, RCT evidence using IBS-SSS or IBS-QoL validated outcomes, publication in GI specialty journals, clinician prescription model, ACG or AASM guideline endorsement, PDT Act enactment (legislative dependency), payer medical necessity policy negotiation. Germany/EU DiGA clearance provides precedent but not US leverage directly.

**Realistic timeline:** US insurance reimbursement for a GI PDT: 5-10 years, contingent on PDT Act passage. Employer benefit integration via CCM bundling: 2-3 years. Self-pay + HSA with LMN: achievable in 12-18 months.

---

### Stress/Fatigue/Recovery (HRV, Readiness Scores, General Wellness)

**Current reimbursement status:** No pathway. All current products in this category (Oura, Whoop, Garmin, Apple Watch wellness features) are self-pay consumer products. HSA/FSA eligible (Oura, Whoop). LSA programs provide employer-funded reimbursement for device purchase.

**Likelihood rating: UNLIKELY for any pathway beyond HSA/FSA and LSA in a 5-year horizon**

**Requirements to achieve reimbursement:** This category faces the deepest structural barrier: there is no ICD-10 diagnosis code for "fatigue," "poor recovery," or "low HRV" that creates medical necessity. To achieve reimbursement, a product would need to (a) target a specific clinical condition (e.g., post-COVID syndrome, burnout as a coded psychiatric condition, cardiac rehabilitation), (b) demonstrate clinical outcomes in an RCT, and (c) secure a DME code or PDT categorization. The pathway most analogous to this would be cardiac rehabilitation monitoring or chronic fatigue syndrome management.

**Realistic timeline:** No realistic insurance reimbursement path without repositioning toward a specific chronic disease. Employer LSA/wellness benefit: immediate. HSA/FSA: immediate (Oura, Whoop already eligible).

---

### General Lifestyle/Preventive Wellness

**Current reimbursement status:** Preventive counseling codes (99401-99404) and Medicare annual wellness visits (G0438, G0439) can incorporate lifestyle discussion but do not reimburse the device itself. ACA preventive services do not cover wellness wearables. General lifestyle tracking apps have no insurance pathway.

**Likelihood rating: UNLIKELY for device/app reimbursement; POSSIBLE for clinician counseling time billed separately**

## SECTION 8: CLINICAL EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

### HSA/FSA and SIGIS Eligibility
- No RCT required.
- Product must qualify as a "medical care" expense under IRS Section 213(d) — i.e., treats, diagnoses, or prevents a specific disease or condition.
- SIGIS (Special Interest Group for IIAS Standards) listing allows automatic FSA/FSA acceptance without LMN.
- LMN from a physician describing specific medical condition can qualify many borderline products.

### Employer Benefit Platform (Maven, Carrot, Progyny)
- Clinical evidence preferred but not mandatory (employer benefits are not regulated like insurance).
- Outcomes data (engagement, symptom improvement, absenteeism, employee satisfaction) are key selling points.
- Peer-reviewed publication helpful; case studies from beta employers can be sufficient for initial contracts.
- Safety and privacy compliance (HIPAA, SOC 2) required.

### RPM (CMS/Medicare)
- FDA-cleared device required (or device that meets FDA "medical device" definition).
- Device must automatically transmit physiologic data (not manual entry).
- Clinician must have an established patient relationship.
- Device must monitor a physiologic parameter relevant to a chronic condition (blood pressure, glucose, SpO2, weight, etc.).
- No specific RCT evidence required by CMS for RPM billing, but commercial payers are applying evidence standards.
- UHC is moving toward requiring evidence of clinical efficacy for RPM coverage decisions.

### DMHT (G0552-G0554 Medicare)
- FDA 510(k) or De Novo clearance required specifically for computerized behavioral therapy for psychiatric disorders.
- Prescription from licensed qualified healthcare professional required.
- Clinician must monitor patient progress and adjust treatment as needed.
- RCT evidence expected by payers considering commercial coverage (though not explicitly required by CMS for Medicare billing).
- Published evidence in validated scores (ISI, PSQI for sleep; PHQ-9, GAD-7 for mental health) strongly recommended for commercial coverage.

### Commercial Payer PDT Coverage (Aetna CPB 0999; Cigna MM-0565; BCBS equivalent)
- FDA clearance or approval required (De Novo, 510(k), or PMA depending on indication).
- Peer-reviewed clinical evidence in indexed journals is required.
- Evidence must show clinical benefit compared to an active control (wait-list controls increasingly insufficient).
- Payers are moving toward requiring RCT data rather than observational studies.
- ICER review strengthens coverage arguments but is not required; ICER has developed an assessment framework for digital health technologies with tiered evidence standards.
- Cost-effectiveness data (QALY analysis) increasingly expected for formal payer submission.
- Prior authorization expected for most commercial coverage policies.

### Traditional DME Insurance Coverage
- Strict Medicare DME criteria: must be durable (withstand repeated use), have therapeutic purpose, be for in-home use, and not be useful to someone in good health.
- HCPCS code assignment required (A-codes or E-codes); new codes require CMS application.
- Clinical evidence showing device is superior to alternatives for a specific condition.
- PDAC (Pricing, Data Analysis and Coding) contractor determination for new devices.

### Key Validated Outcome Instruments by Category
- **Insomnia/Sleep:** Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS), sleep diary metrics.
- **Menopause:** Menopause Rating Scale (MRS), Greene Climacteric Scale, Menopause-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (MENQOL), hot flush frequency/severity diaries.
- **IBS/Digestive:** IBS Symptom Severity Score (IBS-SSS), IBS-QoL, GSRS (GI Symptom Rating Scale), Rome IV criteria documentation.
- **Stress/Fatigue/Recovery:** No standardized reimbursement-level outcome metric exists. Research tools include PROMIS Fatigue, Perceived Stress Scale (PSS), and HRV-based metrics — none accepted by payers as clinical endpoints.
- **Menstrual pain:** NRS/VAS pain scales, Dysmenorrhea Symptom Score.

## SECTION 9: STARTUP STRATEGY RECOMMENDATIONS

### Recommended Sequenced Strategy for a Wearable/App Targeting Menstruation, Menopause, Digestive Discomfort, and Sleep

**Foundational Decision: Prioritize Sleep or Menopause as the Initial Clinical Focus**

Of the four target indications, sleep/insomnia and menopause offer the clearest near-term reimbursement trajectories. Sleep has an active Medicare DMHT coding pathway (G0552-G0554) if the product delivers CBT-I and achieves FDA clearance. Menopause has established employer benefit channels (Carrot, Maven, Progyny) and a validated telehealth insurance model (Gennev, Midi). Digestive and stress/recovery indications have no viable US reimbursement path in the near term and should be positioned as DTC/HSA secondary features.

---

### Phase 1: Immediate Revenue (Months 0-18)

**Primary pathway: DTC self-pay + HSA/FSA + Employer LSA**

1. Launch as a direct-to-consumer subscription or device product, priced at $10-30/month or $150-350 for hardware.
2. Pursue SIGIS-listed FSA/HSA eligibility immediately — this is achievable in 6-12 months and expands the addressable market by 20-30% without requiring FDA clearance. Flo and Clue's paths in 2024-2025 are the playbook.
3. Engage employer benefits brokers and LSA platforms (Forma, Benepass, Espresa) to get listed as an eligible expense in LSA programs. Target employers with 500+ employees and demonstrated interest in women's health benefits.
4. Pursue employer benefit platform partnerships (Carrot Fertility, Maven Clinic, Progyny, Ovia Health) — these platforms are actively looking for clinical tools and pay per-member-per-month fees of $3-15 PMPM for integrated solutions. Focus on the menopause and perimenopause segment where all three platforms are growing rapidly.
5. **Key milestone:** $1-2M ARR from DTC + employer LSA before end of Year 1.

**Revenue estimate Phase 1:** $0.5-3M ARR in Year 1-2, depending on market entry.

---

### Phase 2: Medium-Term Expansion (Months 12-36)

**Primary pathway: Telehealth clinic bundle + employer benefit platform integration**

1. Build a clinical layer: Partner with or build a clinician network capable of supervising wearable data review and providing care management. This enables billing under E&M codes (99213-99215) and CCM codes (99490, 99491) for menopause (N95.1), insomnia (G47.00), and IBS (K58.x) diagnoses.
2. Pursue in-network contracting with Aetna, BCBS, and regional payers for the telehealth visit component. The wearable/monitoring device is bundled into the subscription; the visit is what insurance reimburses. This is the Gennev/Midi Health model.
3. For the sleep indication: pursue FDA 510(k) clearance as a CBT-I digital therapeutic for chronic insomnia disorder. This unlocks the DMHT pathway (G0552-G0554) for Medicare patients, estimated 65+ million beneficiaries. Timeline: 18-30 months from submission.
4. Engage RPM vendor partners (e.g., Tenovi, HealthSnap, Prevounce) to enable automatic data transmission from your wearable to a clinical dashboard. This is required infrastructure for any RPM billing path in Phase 3.
5. Publish first real-world evidence study using validated outcomes instruments (PSQI/ISI for sleep; MRS/MENQOL for menopause) demonstrating symptom improvement vs. usual care. This is the foundation for commercial payer engagement.
6. **Key milestone:** In-network payer contracts for telehealth visits; FDA submission for sleep indication; first peer-reviewed publication.

**Revenue estimate Phase 2:** $3-15M ARR from employer benefit platform contracts + telehealth subscriptions + early insurance billing.

---

### Phase 3: Clinical Reimbursement (Months 30-60+)

**Primary pathway: DMHT reimbursement (sleep) + RPM program (menopause/IBS/chronic management) + payer medical policy**

1. Upon FDA clearance for CBT-I/sleep: immediately begin billing under G0552-G0554 for Medicare patients. This creates the first true insurance-reimbursed revenue stream. Engage commercial payers under Aetna CPB 0999 and Cigna MM-0565 frameworks — both have criteria that a cleared, prescription CBT-I product can meet.
2. Develop an RCT (or multi-site real-world study with active control) for the menopause indication. Target the Greene Climacteric Scale and MRS as primary endpoints. Publish in NAMS (North American Menopause Society) affiliated journals and present at ACOG/NAMS conferences to build specialty society endorsement.
3. Pursue payer medical policy engagement: submit clinical dossiers to Aetna, Cigna, BCBS, and UHC medical policy teams with evidence package. Expect 18-36 months of engagement per payer before formal positive coverage determination.
4. Monitor the PDT Act (H.R. 3288/S. 1702): if enacted, pursue Medicare/Medicaid PDT benefit category for any cleared therapeutic app. Engage Digital Therapeutics Alliance (DTA) for policy advocacy.
5. For the IBS indication: evaluate European market (Germany DiGA pathway) as a parallel revenue stream and evidence-building exercise, since US payer pathway for GI digital therapeutics remains 5-10 years away.
6. Consider pharmacy benefit pathway (following Nerivio/SleepioRx/CVS Caremark model) for scaled distribution: partner with a PBM (CVS Caremark, OptumRx, Express Scripts) to distribute through pharmacy benefit with prior authorization and prescription requirement.
7. **Key milestone:** First commercial payer positive coverage determination; Medicare DMHT revenue live; RCT data published; RPM program operational with clinician network.

**Revenue estimate Phase 3:** $20-100M ARR potential if insurance coverage achieved for 1-2 indications.

### Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial payers restrict RPM (UHC 2026 policy precedent) | High | Do not build business model on RPM billing for wellness indications; focus on telehealth E&M and DMHT codes |
| Pear Therapeutics failure: FDA clearance ≠ payer coverage | High | Secure employer benefit and DTC revenue before investing in FDA clearance; treat clearance as one pathway, not the only pathway |
| DMHT codes restricted to psychiatric disorders only | Medium | Design CBT-I sleep product with psychiatric indication language; or pursue CBT for anxiety/depression comorbid with menopause |
| PDT Act does not pass | Medium | Do not make business model dependent on legislation; use DMHT codes and existing frameworks |
| Evidence bar rising (payers require RCT vs. active control) | High | Begin RCT design in Phase 1 even if underpowered; partner with academic medical center for credibility |
| Consumer wellness wearable market commoditized | Medium | Differentiate via clinician integration and clinical outcomes, not just data collection |
| UHC restricts RPM to 2 conditions | Medium | Focus on Medicare fee-for-service and BCBS/Aetna commercial for RPM if applicable; avoid UHC dependence |
| HSA/FSA eligibility can be rescinded if IRS changes rules | Low | Maintain supplemental revenue streams; don't over-depend on HSA volume |

**Summary of Most Actionable Insights:**

1. **Do not wait for insurance.** Build DTC + HSA/FSA + employer LSA revenue first, targeting $1-5M ARR as proof of demand before major investment in FDA clearance or payer contracting.
2. **Embed in Carrot/Maven/Progyny.** These platforms have enterprise contracts, pay PMPM fees, and are actively integrating wearable tools. This is the fastest path to B2B scaled revenue without insurance.
3. **Sleep is the most reimbursable indication.** The DMHT framework (G0552-G0554) is live, SleepioRx is the reference product, and the FDA clearance pathway for CBT-I is established.
4. **Menopause telehealth is the fastest insurance pathway.** Not the wearable — but a clinician-supervised telehealth service that uses your wearable data as a clinical input, billed under standard E&M codes, can achieve insurance coverage in 2-4 years.
5. **Nerivio is the wearable reimbursement blueprint.** Prescription-required, FDA-cleared, pharmacy-benefit model, payer medical policy engagement over 3-5 years, ~130 million lives covered. This is the 7-10 year target for any wearable seeking true insurance reimbursement.
6. **The PDT Act is the most important legislation to watch.** If enacted, it creates the first direct Medicare/Medicaid benefit category for FDA-authorized PDTs beyond the psychiatric/behavioral health restriction — which would enable IBS, menopause, and other indications to achieve reimbursement through an established framework.
